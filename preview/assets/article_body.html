Overnight buses will run on three routes in a six-month trial

Dana ReyesSeptember 17, 2026

The city council voted 7 to 2 on Tuesday to fund overnight bus service on three routes, starting in November. Buses will run every 30 minutes between midnight and 5 a.m. on the Harbor, Eastgate, and Mill Street lines.

The trial will cost $2.4 million over six months, paid mostly from a state transit grant. Council member Ruth Alvarez, who sponsored the measure, said the routes were chosen because they connect the city's two hospitals, the port, and the largest warehouse district.

A city survey of 1,800 night-shift workers found that 61 percent commute more than 40 minutes each way, and that one in four has turned down a shift because they had no way to get home. "People are sleeping in their cars in hospital parking lots between shifts," Alvarez said. "That is a transit problem, and we can fix it."

The two members who voted no said the city should wait for ridership data from a smaller pilot. Council member Tom Becker argued that the survey overstated demand because it was distributed through employers who support the service.

Transit officials said they will publish ridership numbers every month. If average ridership falls below 12 passengers per trip by April, the council will vote on whether to end the trial early.
