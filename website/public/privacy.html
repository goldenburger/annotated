Privacy policy

Updated September 20, 2026

annotated is a Chrome extension and website for writing short takes on passages, clips and posts from around the web. This page explains what it collects and why.

When you sign in with Google

annotated receives your name, email address and profile photo from Google. Your name, photo and handle appear on your annotations and comments. Your email address is used only to sign you in and is never shown to anyone.

What you publish

Annotations you publish are public. That includes your take, its tag and poll, the passage, clip, audio or screenshot you captured, and a link to the source. Comments, reactions and poll votes are public too. You can edit or delete your own annotations and comments at any time, and deleting an annotation also deletes its files.

What stays on your computer

Annotations you save while signed out stay in your browser and are never uploaded. Your display settings are also kept only in your browser.

Claims

If you file a claim about an annotation, your name, email address and what you wrote are stored so the claim can be reviewed. They are not shown publicly.

What annotated does not do

annotated does not sell your information, show ads, or track you across other websites. The extension runs on the pages you visit so it can offer the Annotate button beside text you select, but it sends nothing anywhere unless you capture and publish something.

Services annotated uses

To clip a podcast, the extension searches Apple's public podcast directory with the episode's title and downloads the part of the episode's audio you clip from the show's own server. To show a post from X as an embed, the page loads X's embed. Google handles sign-in.

Where it is stored

Accounts, annotations and files are stored with Supabase in the United States. The website is hosted by Netlify.
